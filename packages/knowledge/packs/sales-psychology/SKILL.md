# Sales

The offer names who it is for, what they get, and the one next step. If a part is missing, leave it blank. Do not invent it to make the page sound finished.

## Proof

Proof comes only from the user: a name, a quote, a logo, a result, or a price they wrote down. Do not invent a testimonial, a customer count, a logo strip, a star rating, or a review. If the proof is missing, leave the block out.

## Principles

Reciprocity, commitment, social proof, authority, liking, and scarcity are a paraphrase of Robert Cialdini's book Influence. Use one only with material the user already gave. Reciprocity means give something useful before the ask. Commitment means a small step the visitor chose. Social proof means a real customer the user named. Authority means a credential the user holds. Liking means plain speech, not fake warmth. Scarcity means a limit only when that limit is real.

Do not attach a lift or a percent to any principle. If a source has no number, this pack has no number.

## Dark patterns

No dark patterns. No fake countdown, and no timer that resets when it expires. No pre-checked extra. No cost that appears only at the last step. No item added without a clear yes. No button that shames a refusal. Urgency is allowed only when the brief states a real deadline.

## Price and risk

Show the price the user set. Do not invent a discount or a crossed-out comparison. A guarantee appears only when the user stated one.

## Page shape

A sales page states the offer and one next step. A funnel page has one step, and the next page is that step, not a second offer. A page that asks for a call, a reservation, or a sign-up uses the same rule: one real action, and proof only from the user.
